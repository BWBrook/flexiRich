# Fable follow-up: verified observations and unresolved mechanisms

on 5 October 2026. Their received snapshots and exact checking script are in
`evidence/`. The checking script replayed byte-for-byte against its original

Only already-open compact Judbury tables were used. This local account is not
a posted reply or a declaration that the disagreement is resolved.

Fable correctly identifies weak summer support in the first training year:
172 station-days versus 620 and 819 in subsequent summers. Of those first-year
days, 91 came from co3. Annual bird variation and changes in rates at fixed
stations deserve explicit competitors. These observations do not establish
how much of the five-versus-about-two discovery discrepancy they explain.
The new species are very sparse, and no fitted seasonal discovery model was
evaluated in that review.

The reported station-period rates count weekly positive cells per station-day.
They combine species occurrence, encounter intensity, detection, observation
rules and weekly-binary saturation. The largest co3 mammal contrast selects
6 positive cells in 91 days against 21 in 35 days, among many inspected
station/block contrasts. It is evidence of changing recorded encounters,
not identified camera detectability or grounds to revise effort.

The strong annual bird association uses three species selected for frequent
detection. The lag-52 odds ratios were approximately 5.40 for forest raven,
1.95 for common bronzewing and 1.56 for grey currawong; omitting raven reduced
the pooled estimate from 3.26 to 1.77 in the independent follow-up. These
findings motivate the harmonic comparator, but do not establish a shared
seasonal transfer law for all 24 never-recorded birds.

Within-station and cross-station odds ratios do not partition a variance or
measure the fraction of clustering that is local. They condition on different
sets of observations and use overlapping pairs. Cross-station association
remained after placement conditioning and diminished further with season or
13-week blocks; bird and mammal signals differed. Same-station cross-species
association also diminished after block conditioning. Both array and local
persistence therefore remain candidates, alongside calendar and shared
station effects. Agreement among agents is not independent field validation.

Recorded camera moves do not explain the Goal012 residual clustering. The
claim that none of the three missing species was at a moved camera needs a
timing qualification: the dog record was at co3's earlier placement, long
before that station moved. Movement is not a sufficient explanation for its
long absence, but the nominal camera did eventually move.

The approved comparison now keeps placement factors common to all arms and
tests both spatial scales of persistence against a harmonic. A separate
shared-service stress tests whether omitted station-level variation produces
spurious temporal selection. A real estimated service-period model remains
part of the next nuisance-estimation stage; it has not been declared supported
by these summaries or silently equated with camera hardware effects.

Barry has been asked about consistency of bird classification/human review,
dated co2/co3 field or equipment notes beyond the documented events, and
monitoring after May 2022. Those answers affect empirical interpretation and
future validation. The synthetic discrimination work can proceed meanwhile.
Barry's independent wombat information concerns a location about 1 km away,
not a confirmed prior record at Judbury itself.
